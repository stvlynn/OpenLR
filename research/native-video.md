# Native video verification

The 2026-11-06 [native video smoke](../scripts/native-video-smoke.mjs) generates a three-second VP9/Opus WebM. Its consecutive one-second segments pair red, green, and blue frames with 330, 660, and 990 Hz tones. The real desktop CLI imports the clip, seeks, advances playback, pauses, trims to 1.2–2.7 seconds, rotates the frames, and writes previews, a saved catalog frame, and the trimmed WebM. A separate Chrome page uses the bundled MediaBunny sinks and Chrome's decoders to inspect the written files. It checks the saved PNG against the paused preview and samples two frames and two windows of decoded PCM in the trim. The test also checks that the exports preserve the selected asset, paused playback, and catalog edit, and that exporting the original returns the same bytes as the source fixture.

A follow-up source review on 2026-11-07 examined the possible handoff from the playback iterator to the paused-frame read. Context7's MediaBunny guide describes sink calls as independent (except an enabled canvas pool) and requires returning manually consumed iterators. OpenLR already calls `frames.return()` and does not enable pooling. The pinned 1.65.1 source confirms separate decoder variables in `mediaSamplesInRange` and `mediaSamplesAtTimestamps`; `CanvasSink.getCanvas` and `canvases` delegate to these separate paths. Their `return()` methods mark termination, wake queues and close buffered samples, while decoder closure occurs in the background pump's `finally`. Thus merely awaiting `return()` is not evidence of a completed native-decoder shutdown and would not establish a repair. Pending work can overlap, but no causal link to the pause failure has yet been observed. No serialization or delay is added on this hypothesis. [Upstream sink guide](https://github.com/vanilagy/mediabunny/blob/main/docs/guide/media-sinks.md) · Local inspected source: `node_modules/mediabunny/src/media-sink.ts`, version 1.65.1 (the Context7 catalog separately lists 1.63.0, so local source controls version-specific conclusions).
